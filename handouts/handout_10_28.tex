\subsubsection{Addition}
\textit{Sa\tsb{g}ayt} and \textit{jep} are both used as equality terms for addition. What's interesting is that \textit{Sa\tsb{g}ayt} is not self-referrential
\begin{exe}
\ex \begin{xlist}
\item k'i \tsb{g}an=hl k'i ii sa\tsb{g}ayt gilbil-t\\
 one \Pcnj{}=\Cn{} one and all.together two-3.\Ii{}\\
'One and one is two'
\item \gll k'i=hl \tsb{g}an=hl k'i ii sa\tsb{g}ayt gilbil-t\\
one=\Cn{} \Pcnj{}=\Cn{} one and all.together two-3.\Ii{}\\
\textit{Consultant Comments: "This would mean there are 4 total"}
\end{xlist}
\end{exe}
The variation in connectives needs to be re-elicited with more precision on where the predicate is.

\begin{exe}
\ex \gll k'i \tsb{g}an=hl k'i an-jep=hl gilbil\\
one \Pcnj{}=\Cn{} one ??-make=\Cn{} two\\
'One and one makes two'
\end{exe}
No explicit comments here that there are 4 total, but this hasn't rigourously been tested.

An example of a context that failed to get the wanted morphemes:
\textit{we had 5 people coming for dinner. I only had 2 chairs and you added 3 more}
\begin{exe}
\ex \gll k'iinam-'y gilbil ha'nii ta'a loo'n  ii heksamox dim-'n heksamox gitnam gwile' ha'nii ta'a loo'n\\
give-1.\Ii{} two chair {} 2.\Prep{} \Ccnj{} again \Fut{}-1.\I{} again give\{1.\Ii{}\} three chair {} 2.\Prep{}\\
'I gave you 2 chairs and I'll give you three more' \textit{VG}
\end{exe}

\end{exe}
\begin{exe}
\ex \gll t\tsb{x}alp\tsb{x} \tsb{g}abi dim-'n dip besa\tsb{x}an-t ehl sa'ap dip\\
four \Cnt{} \Fut{}-1Pl.\I{} 1Pl.\I{} separate-3.\Ii{} ?? around 1Pl.\Ii{}(?)\\
